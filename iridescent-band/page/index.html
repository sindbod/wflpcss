What the photo shows

  • Structure12 staggered rows of brick cells on a 1.8 mm row pitch. Bricks are about 3.7 × 1.5 mm, 4.3 mm apart, with a half-brick offset.
  • EdgesA sage binding runs along the top. The lowest three rows shrink and break up, a knitted halftone fade.
  • ColourPastel only, saturation 0.1–0.25. The hue swings between magenta and cream about every 20° of surface angle.
  • ReadingMagenta sits where a softbox mirrors in the band, cream where it does not. That points to an interference layer over light yarn. Best fit: a TiO₂-like film about 106 nm thick.
  • LightingKey softbox about 40° camera-left, a rim light behind left, a weaker fill on the right and nothing on the camera axis. The default studio rig recreates this.

Using this to train the dynamic behaviour

Settle the physics first

Shine a phone torch on the real band in a dark room and tilt it. If you see clean spectral rainbows that race across the band, it is a holographic foil: use approach 3. If the colour slides smoothly between magenta and cream, it is an interference layer: approaches 2, 4 and 5. The Flashlight test lighting preset shows both cases.

Calibrate on a few real captures

Shoot the garment under a single known light at three or four angles, set the same light here, and tune film thickness, reflectance gain and roughness until the renders match. These are the parameters a model needs to see varied.

Generate in bulk, validate at the top

Export randomised sets from approach 2 (thousands of views, with masks and camera labels). Check them against approach 4 or the Cycles frames, which add global illumination and true relief, so the model does not learn shortcuts specific to a raster shader.

Scale the offline set when needed

The repository includes blender/generate_dataset.py, which renders domain-randomised Cycles frames with masks and the same JSON labels. It runs in 20 s per frame on a laptop CPU, or seconds on a GPU.

Built with three.js r186, three-gpu-pathtracer 0.0.26, fflate and Blender 5.2 Cycles. Outdoor and night HDRIs are CC0 from Poly Haven, via the three.js examples; the studio, dark-room and neon rigs are generated.
