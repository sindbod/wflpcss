What the renders show

Six findings

  1. 0 mm

    On-camera flash casts no seam shadows

    The flash sits by the lens, so shadows fall behind what casts them. Your photo has none. A hard strobe 45° off-axis gives crisp lines, but only 1–2 mm wide.

  2. 21–32 %

    Skin under the net reads darker than the net is open

    The net is 50 % open, yet fair skin behind it keeps only this share of its light. Light crosses the net twice, and skin spreads it sideways in between. An alpha blend makes the mesh too transparent.

  3. 45 → 85 %

    Angle closes the net, tension barely opens it

    Coverage rises from face-on to 75° because the yarn has thickness. Stretching it 40 % opens it only from 50 % to 44 %.

  4. 0th order

    The briefing's holo is a diffraction foil

    White glare with violet nearest it is a grating's zeroth and first order. A clear film on bright metal stays near silver with a faint tint.

  5. +2.2 dB

    Frequency separation needs level matching

    Pasting pass-1 threads back helps only if the detail is scaled to the new lighting, the source is pixel-aligned, and the holo stays out. The plain high-pass lost up to 21 dB on the renders.

  6. [PROD_*]

    Tokens are inert until trained

    A base model reads them as noise. The generator writes token-captioned frames with masks for a LoRA, and the refiner graph loads it.

Four effects, isolated

Seam shadows: on-camera flash vs hard strobe

A 100 mm close-up of the left channel's foot, the bottom band and the bare skin below it. Under the near-coaxial flash nothing casts a visible shadow. The strobe 45° to the left throws a hairline under the band and channel: they sit 1.7–1.8 mm off the skin, so even 45° light only moves their shadow by about that much. At catalogue distance that is less than a pixel.

How open the net is: viewing angle vs tension

Coverage of this tulle (0.6 mm cells, 0.155 mm yarn, 0.09 mm thick) from the OSL net shader, which widens each yarn's footprint by its thickness times tan θ along the ray. It explains the darker lower belly in your photo and the opaque, glowing edges under rim light. Tension, at right, opens the cells but not the yarn: on fair skin under softboxes the panel's see-through only rises from 0.25 to 0.28 between 0 and 40 % strain.

Why skin under the net reads darker than the net is open

Here the net is made black, so the panel shows only skin light that got through it, as a share of the bare skin (front panel, linear light). Light crosses the net twice. Under softboxes the way in and the way out use unrelated openings, so the share is about t² whatever the skin does. Under on-camera flash both crossings could use the same opening, and opaque skin would keep 0.42. Real skin carries light millimetres sideways under its surface, so fair skin drops to 0.32; deep skin, which scatters less far, to 0.37. The QA node in the refiner checks generated images against this range.

Three holographic models under flash and rim light

Diffraction foil (one grating along the band), glitter foil (0.8 mm cells with random grating directions) and a TiO₂-like film on aluminium. Under the flash, seen from the front, only the gratings give white glare with spectral fringes. Under rim light, seen from the side where the strip light reflects in the band, the gratings show spectral tints and sparkle while the film stays silver-white. The table shows why: the brighter the metal, the weaker the film's colour.

Post-AI step

Frequency separation, scored on ground truth

The macro camera rendered under the flash stands in for the pass-1 output; the same camera under softboxes is the target; the target with its weave blurred away is the refiner's output. Each variant restores detail from the flash frame and is scored against the target. Detail error is the RMS of the detail band (lower is better); PSNR covers colour too (higher is better).

All skin tones and lighting changes

Mesh PSNR gained over no restore, for 3 skin tones × 6 lighting changes (flash ↔ softbox, strobe → softbox, rim ↔ softbox, flash → rim). Where the refined frame is darker than the source, the plain high-pass pastes bright threads into it; where the source is dark, the log split blows its ratios up.

The refiner, rebuilt

Pass 1 can be any VTON model; its output and a token mask go in. Core nodes plus four Sheer VTON nodes, checked against ComfyUI's own node definitions (inputs, outputs and link types). The diffusion steps were not run here.

SheerFrequencyRestore

Restores the weave from the pass-1 image, with the detail scaled to the refined image's local brightness, the radius taken from the weave period, a highlight guard and an exclude mask for the holo. Refuses sources that are not the same size as the refined image.

SheerTokenPrompt

Builds the refiner prompt from the tokens, the lighting scenario, the skin tone and the holo model, in the briefing's wording or the corrected wording.

SheerMaskSplit

Turns a token mask image into mesh, lace, holo, mesh + holo and garment masks.

SheerTransmissionQA

Measures how much skin shows through the mesh relative to bare skin and reports whether it reads physically (t²) or like an alpha blend (t).

Limits

What this does and doesn't show

  • No diffusion model ran. This sandbox has no GPU, and Hugging Face and Civitai are blocked by its network policy. The pass-2 settings (denoise 0.25–0.35, ControlNet weights) are untested. The graph is only checked for structure, against ComfyUI's own node definitions.
  • The renders are path-traced physics, not photographs. The mesh, yarn and fair skin are fitted to one editorial photo whose colour grade is unknown. The medium and deep skin albedos are literature-style estimates, not measurements.
  • The body is a smooth dress form. Real bodies add curvature, folds and fabric that lifts off the skin; lifted fabric is where seam shadows grow wider than 2 mm.
  • The diffraction foil uses a paraxial lobe model: exact for light returning to its source, as with the flash, and approximate elsewhere. The lace is procedural, not a scan of the product.
  • Try-on of intimate apparel should only run on images of adult models who agreed to that use. Keep the pipeline away from arbitrary uploaded photos of people.
